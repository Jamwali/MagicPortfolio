I built a retrieval-augmented chatbot over restaurant reviews with one hard boundary: the complete interaction had to stay on the machine. No hosted language model, no remote vector database, and no source text leaving the device.

That boundary was useful. It turned privacy from a sentence in a policy into a property of the architecture.

## The path through the system

The application follows a compact retrieval path:

1. The question becomes an embedding.
2. ChromaDB searches the review embeddings for the five nearest matches.
3. LangChain places those matches and the question into a grounded prompt.
4. llama3.2 generates the answer through Ollama.

Retrieval is doing more than supplying extra text. It constrains the model to the small part of the corpus that is most likely to contain evidence for the question.

## Latency is part of correctness

The system responds in under two seconds on the target setup. That number mattered because question answering is conversational: a correct response that arrives too slowly still breaks the experience.

Working locally also makes the trade-off visible. Larger contexts can provide more evidence, but they increase prompt size and generation time. Retrieving five matches was a practical balance for this corpus—not a universal constant.

## “Grounded” is not the same as “guaranteed”

Retrieval-augmented generation can still fail in at least two distinct ways. Retrieval may miss the relevant review, or generation may misuse the context it receives. A smooth answer does not reveal which part went wrong.

The next version should make that boundary easier to inspect. I would show the supporting review snippets beside the answer and build a small evaluation set for retrieval hit rate and answer faithfulness.

Local inference solved the privacy constraint. The next challenge is making the system’s evidence as visible as its answer.
